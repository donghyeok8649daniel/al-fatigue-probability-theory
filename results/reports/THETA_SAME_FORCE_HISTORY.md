# Same-force $P$–$u$–$\Theta$ history test

## Result

The deterministic conservative 1D generalized-LJ chain reaches different
reduced states on loading and unloading even when the externally applied normal
force is the same:

$$
\boxed{
(P,u,\Theta)_{\rm load}(Q^*)\neq
(P,u,\Theta)_{\rm unload}(Q^*)
}
$$

for every force level tested below.

This establishes **dynamic history dependence / non-retracing of the reduced
state** for the tested finite-chain protocol. It does **not** establish
irreversible G3 dissipation, because the source chain is conservative.

---

## Protocol

- source model: conservative 1D normal generalized-LJ chain;
- atoms: 512;
- dimensionless time step: $dt^*=0.01$;
- stress-to-force mapping: $Q=\sigma/E$ with $E=69$ GPa;
- mean stress: 100 MPa;
- stress amplitude: 100 MPa, giving a 0--200 MPa pulsating tensile cycle;
- dimensionless angular frequency: $\omega^*=0.02$;
- smooth loading ramp: two cycles;
- comparison cycle: cycle 4;
- bulk comparison: two boundary spacings excluded;
- finite-$M$ field estimator: a common-grid Gaussian kernel with bandwidth
  $1.5$ times the Silverman rule.

The Gaussian kernel is only a numerical estimator of the finite empirical
measure. It is not a physical Gaussian PDF assumption.

The chosen $\omega^*=0.02$ is inherited from the earlier proof-of-principle
Theta reconstruction. It must not be identified with a laboratory fatigue
frequency.

## Same-force comparisons

The loading and unloading times were paired so that the external force agrees
to approximately $10^{-7}$ in the dimensionless force coordinate. The small
remaining mismatch is caused by the finite integration time step.

| Force level | $L^1(P_L,P_U)$ | KS distance | normalized $u$ difference | normalized $\Theta$ difference | $\bar a_L$ | $\bar a_U$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 MPa | 0.202176 | 0.101031 | 0.266952 | 0.481362 | 1.002442196 | 1.002822239 |
| 150 MPa | 0.702285 | 0.206064 | 1.406114 | 1.827638 | 1.002535945 | 1.002878285 |
| 186.6 MPa | 1.168800 | 0.348864 | 1.520421 | 1.728824 | 1.002656255 | 1.002857023 |
| 50 MPa | 0.960826 | 0.331163 | 1.326338 | 1.746931 | 1.002082085 | 1.002689534 |

The $L^1$ values use normalized KDE estimates on the same grid. The $u$ and
$\Theta$ differences are weighted RMS differences over the common state-space
support and are normalized by the corresponding local velocity or conditional
variance scale.

Thus the state difference is not carried by the mean spacing alone. The
spacing density, conditional mean velocity, and conditional velocity variance
all distinguish the load and unload passages.

## Direct interpretation of $\Theta$

The active definition is

$$
\boxed{
\Theta(a,t)=\operatorname{Var}(\dot a_i\mid a_i=a).
}
$$

For the unit-mass normalized chain,

$$
\mathbb E\!\left[\frac12\dot a^2\middle|a\right]
=\frac12\left[u(a,t)^2+\Theta(a,t)\right].
$$

Therefore the observed same-force difference in $\Theta$ means that two states
with the same external force can carry different conditional kinetic spreads in
spacing phase space.

This is precisely the kind of information lost if the state is reduced to the
instantaneous stress or even to the mean spacing alone.

## Estimator sensitivity

The Gaussian bandwidth factor was swept from 1.0 to 2.0. The magnitude of the
finite-sample distances changes, as expected, but the load/unload distinction
does not disappear.

For example, the density $L^1$ distances across bandwidth factors
$1.0,1.25,1.5,1.75,2.0$ are

- 100 MPa: 0.253351, 0.222856, 0.202176, 0.191116, 0.180007;
- 150 MPa: 0.810158, 0.756607, 0.702285, 0.648241, 0.595091;
- 186.6 MPa: 1.336075, 1.252672, 1.168800, 1.084794, 1.001000;
- 50 MPa: 1.091214, 1.028173, 0.960826, 0.890929, 0.820577.

Hence the result is not created by one particular bandwidth choice, although
all quantitative KDE distances remain finite-sample estimator dependent.

## Conservative work balance: why this is not yet G3

For each cycle, the external boundary-work increment was compared with the
change in total mechanical energy of the chain.

| Cycle | external work increment | mechanical-energy increment | difference |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $1.2331305363\times10^{-5}$ | $1.2331305163\times10^{-5}$ | $2.00\times10^{-13}$ |
| 2 | $5.1005210722\times10^{-4}$ | $5.1005210767\times10^{-4}$ | $-4.46\times10^{-13}$ |
| 3 | $1.0028312061\times10^{-3}$ | $1.0028312085\times10^{-3}$ | $-2.39\times10^{-12}$ |
| 4 | $9.7892386106\times10^{-4}$ | $9.7892386430\times10^{-4}$ | $-3.24\times10^{-12}$ |
| 5 | $3.8416871079\times10^{-4}$ | $3.8416871311\times10^{-4}$ | $-2.32\times10^{-12}$ |
| 6 | $1.1513102405\times10^{-5}$ | $1.1513103352\times10^{-5}$ | $-9.48\times10^{-13}$ |

Thus the apparent loop/state non-retracing is generated by inertia, wave
propagation, and phase-space redistribution in a conservative driven system.
The work is retained as mechanical energy rather than being identified with a
positive irreversible dissipation rate.

Accordingly,

$$
\boxed{
\text{same-force non-retracing}
\not\Rightarrow
\dot D_{\rm irr}>0.
}
$$

G3 still requires a mechanically justified irreversible mechanism.

## Relation to the exact Theta shape identity

The previously verified exact relation remains

$$
\boxed{
\Theta\,\partial_a\ln P
=\mathcal A-D_tu-\partial_a\Theta.
}
$$

The present calculation adds a different result: the mechanically generated
fields entering that identity are themselves path dependent at fixed external
force.

Therefore a memoryless scalar constitutive reduction

$$
(P,u,\Theta)=\mathcal S[Q(t)]
$$

is falsified by this finite-chain protocol.

The correct active chain is instead

$$
\boxed{
\text{LJ mechanics}
\to F_M(a,c,t)
\to\{P(a,t),u(a,t),\Theta(a,t)\}
\to\{\bar a,\bar U,\text{first passage}\}.
}
$$

---

## Remaining physical blocker

This result is a proof of dynamic state memory on the microscopic finite-chain
time scale. Earlier time-scale analysis showed a very large separation between
ordinary conservative lattice dynamics and laboratory fatigue frequencies.
Therefore the present non-retracing cannot yet be claimed as the physical
Hz-scale fatigue hysteresis mechanism.

The next physical requirement is to identify a mechanics-consistent slow or
irreversible mechanism that can preserve or convert the already established
$P$--$u$--$\Theta$ path dependence at laboratory fatigue time scales, without
replacing it by an empirical memory variable or arbitrary stochastic PDF.
