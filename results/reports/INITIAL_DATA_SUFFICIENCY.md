# Initial-data sufficiency audit

## Question

Does the one-point initial spacing distribution `P0(lambda)` uniquely determine the later spacing probability under the same deterministic generalized-LJ dynamics and the same loading?

A stronger version is also tested: is the one-point initial phase-space measure `F0(lambda,c)` sufficient?

## Setup

Two eight-spacing chains use exactly the same multiset of initial spacing/rate pairs but a different spatial ordering.

Chain A:

`[0.98, 0.99, 1.00, 1.01, 1.02, 1.005, 0.995, 1.015]`

Chain B:

`[0.98, 1.02, 1.00, 0.995, 0.99, 1.015, 1.01, 1.005]`

All initial spacing rates are zero. Hence the empirical one-point measures `P0(lambda)` and `F0(lambda,c)` are exactly identical.

The active normalized generalized-LJ model is used with

- `m = 12.19`
- `n = 6`
- fixed left boundary
- zero right external force for this audit
- `dt = 1e-4`
- final normalized time `tau = 1`

The two chains differ only in the ordering of neighboring spacings.

## Analytic local check

For

$$
M_g(t)=\frac1M\sum_i g(\lambda_i(t))
$$

and `g(lambda)=lambda^2`, zero initial spacing rates give

$$
M_g''(0)
=\frac{2}{M}\sum_i\lambda_i(0)\ddot\lambda_i(0).
$$

Because `ddot lambda_i` depends on neighboring spacings, the two spatial permutations give different values even though their one-point `F0` is identical:

- Chain A: `-3.512988749e-3`
- Chain B: `-1.856526469e-3`

Thus the later one-point probability state cannot be a unique functional of one-point `F0` alone in the exact finite chain.

## Direct integration

At `tau = 1`, the empirical spacing distributions differ with

- empirical two-sample KS distance: `0.375`
- maximum difference between sorted spacing samples: `0.0103663151`
- mean spacing A: `1.0011877327`
- mean spacing B: `1.0015808094`
- variance A: `7.328110645e-5`
- variance B: `1.816367517e-5`

The difference is not caused by different initial one-point distributions or different loading; it is generated by the neighbor ordering that the one-point marginals discarded.

## Conclusion

The exact information hierarchy for the active finite chain is

$$
\text{ordered }\{\lambda_i(0),c_i(0)\}
\Longleftrightarrow\Gamma_0
\Longrightarrow F_0(\lambda,c)
\Longrightarrow P_0(\lambda).
$$

The reverse implications do not hold in general.

Therefore:

- given a fully specified deterministic microscopic initial state, `P0` and `F0` are exact push-forwards and are easy to construct;
- `P0 + sigma(t)` is not generally enough for unique prediction;
- even one-point `F0 + sigma(t)` is not generally enough because neighbor correlations are missing;
- the ordered microscopic state, or an ensemble measure over the full labeled state, remains the exact reference initialization.

This does not prevent a useful reduced initialization. It means that any reduced closure must explicitly state the assumption used to reconstruct or neglect the missing spatial correlations.
