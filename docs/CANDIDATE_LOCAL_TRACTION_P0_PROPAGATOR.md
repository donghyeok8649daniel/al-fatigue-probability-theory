# Candidate local-traction propagator from initial P0 to later P

Status: **CANDIDATE REDUCED MODEL — not yet promoted to the active exact finite-chain theory.**

Purpose: satisfy the hard modeling requirement

$$
P_0(a) + \sigma_n(x,0:t) \longrightarrow P_a(a,t\mid x)
$$

without resolving individual atom positions.

## 1. Why a new modeling level is necessary

The finite nearest-neighbor generalized-LJ chain does **not** admit a universal exact map from the one-point initial spacing density alone. Two microscopic chains can have the same $P_0$ and the same loading history but different neighbor ordering and therefore different later $P$. The existing initial-data and pair-state counterexamples establish this no-go result.

Therefore any model that uses only $P_0$ must introduce an explicit reduction principle. The present candidate uses a **local prescribed-traction reduction** rather than an arbitrary named PDF or an arbitrary diffusion law.

The reduction assumption is:

> At a material point $x$, the continuum/FEM normal stress history $\sigma_n(x,t)$ is supplied to the probability model as the local generalized traction. Explicit atomic-neighbor trajectories are not resolved inside that local constitutive unit.

This is not claimed to be an exact projection of the finite chain. It is a candidate local constitutive model that must be validated against higher-fidelity simulations and experiment.

## 2. Local representative spacing mechanics

Let $a$ be a local inter-layer spacing, $a_0$ its reference value, and

$$
\lambda=\frac{a}{a_0}.
$$

Let $\mu_a$ be the effective inertia associated with the local relative-spacing coordinate. For a chosen representative two-plane relative coordinate, $\mu_a$ is the corresponding reduced inertia; it must be derived from the kinetic unit used in the local model rather than fitted solely to obtain a desired fatigue time scale.

Use the same intrinsic normal interaction energy as the active theory,

$$
U(a)=EA_0a_0\,\phi(\lambda),
$$

with

$$
\phi(\lambda)
=\frac{\lambda^{-m}}{m(m-n)}
-\frac{\lambda^{-n}}{n(m-n)}.
$$

The local prescribed-traction equation is

$$
\mu_a\ddot a=A_0\sigma_n(x,t)-\frac{dU}{da}.
$$

Define the local time scale

$$
t_{0,a}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_a a_0}{EA_0}},
$$

nondimensional time $\tau=t/t_{0,a}$, spacing rate

$$
c=\frac{d\lambda}{d\tau},
$$

and the existing stress bridge

$$
q(x,\tau)=\frac{\sigma_n(x,t)}{E}.
$$

Then

$$
\frac{d\lambda}{d\tau}=c,
$$

$$
\frac{dc}{d\tau}=q(x,\tau)-\phi'(\lambda).
$$

No neighboring atom index appears in this reduced local equation; neighbor mechanics are replaced by the supplied local traction field. This replacement is the central closure assumption and must remain explicit.

## 3. Phase-space transport

Let $F(\lambda,c,\tau\mid x)$ be the local spacing/rate phase-space density. The characteristic equations above generate the exact Liouville transport equation for this reduced model:

$$
\partial_\tau F
+\partial_\lambda(cF)
+\partial_c\left([q(x,\tau)-\phi'(\lambda)]F\right)=0.
$$

The spacing density is

$$
P(\lambda,\tau\mid x)=\int F(\lambda,c,\tau\mid x)\,dc.
$$

This equation does not assume Gaussian, Weibull, Boltzmann, or any other named density family.

## 4. How P0 alone initializes the candidate

For an initially static preparation, impose the physical preparation condition

$$
c(0)=0.
$$

Then the phase-space initial condition is fixed by the spacing density alone:

$$
F_0(\lambda,c\mid x)=P_0(\lambda\mid x)\,\delta(c).
$$

Thus, under the stated static-preparation assumption,

$$
P_0(\lambda\mid x)+q(x,0:\tau)
$$

is sufficient to determine the later reduced phase-space density and therefore $P(\lambda,\tau\mid x)$.

This does **not** mean that $P(\lambda,\tau_1)$ alone is a sufficient restart state at an arbitrary intermediate time $\tau_1$. The hidden rate information at that time is retained internally by the characteristic flow. The required map is from the original $P_0$ plus the full stress history to the requested later $P$.

## 5. Direct push-forward form

Let

$$
\Lambda(\tau;\lambda_0,x)
$$

be the spacing characteristic generated by

$$
\Lambda(0;\lambda_0,x)=\lambda_0,
$$

$$
C(0;\lambda_0,x)=0.
$$

Then the later spacing density is the direct push-forward

$$
P(\lambda,\tau\mid x)
=\int P_0(\lambda_0\mid x)
\,\delta[\lambda-\Lambda(\tau;\lambda_0,x)]\,d\lambda_0.
$$

If the map $\lambda_0\mapsto\Lambda(\tau;\lambda_0,x)$ is locally invertible,

$$
P(\lambda,\tau\mid x)
=\sum_{\lambda_{0,k}:\Lambda(\tau;\lambda_{0,k},x)=\lambda}
\frac{P_0(\lambda_{0,k}\mid x)}
{|\partial\Lambda/\partial\lambda_0|_{\lambda_{0,k}}}.
$$

This is the desired reduced propagator:

$$
P(\tau)=\mathcal T_{\tau,0}^{q}[P_0].
$$

It can be evaluated by characteristics on an initial-spacing quadrature grid rather than by storing an ordered atomic chain.

## 6. Exact energy identity inside the candidate

For one characteristic, define the nondimensional intrinsic mechanical energy

$$
e^*(\tau)=\frac12c^2+\phi(\lambda).
$$

Then

$$
\frac{de^*}{d\tau}
=q(x,\tau)c.
$$

Therefore the candidate preserves the same distinction already used in the active theory: external work can change conservative mechanical energy, but this does not by itself create thermodynamic irreversible dissipation.

## 7. Tail and first passage

Any spacing threshold $\lambda_*$ can be applied directly to the pushed-forward density:

$$
Q_*(\tau)=\int_{\lambda_*}^{\infty}P(\lambda,\tau)\,d\lambda.
$$

The characteristic first-passage time is

$$
\tau^*(\lambda_0)=\inf\{\tau:\Lambda(\tau;\lambda_0)\ge\lambda_*\}.
$$

The cumulative first-passage mass is therefore

$$
F_*(\tau)
=\int P_0(\lambda_0)
I[\tau^*(\lambda_0)\le\tau]\,d\lambda_0.
$$

The actual crack-initiation threshold $\lambda_c$ from the active normal-instability criterion can be substituted for $\lambda_*$ when the model is validated in that regime.

## 8. What the numerical audit shows

A diagnostic audit on `numerical-fem` uses an explicitly specified bounded initial spacing set only to test the propagator mechanics. It is **not** proposed as the physical aluminum $P_0$.

For $m=12.19$, $n=6$, mean stress and amplitude each mapped from 100 MPa through $q=\sigma/E$, $\omega^*=0.02$, and a diagnostic threshold $\lambda_*=1.004$:

- the same applied stress value at different phases gives strongly different spacing distributions;
- cycle-end snapshot tail mass is non-monotone;
- cumulative first-passage mass is monotone;
- per-characteristic external work and intrinsic mechanical-energy change agree to approximately $2.3\times10^{-10}$ maximum absolute error in the audit;
- the map is generated entirely from the specified $P_0$, the static initial-rate condition, and the stress history.

These results establish internal consistency of the candidate propagator, not validation against real aluminum fatigue data.

## 9. Critical limitation

If

$$
P_0(\lambda)=\delta(\lambda-1)
$$

and all characteristics have the same initial rate and receive exactly the same local stress history, every characteristic remains identical. The density therefore remains a delta measure and no probability tail can be generated from nothing.

Hence a useful fatigue application requires at least one physically justified source of initial/local spread, for example a measured or independently derived non-degenerate $P_0$, or a spatially varying supplied stress field. The origin of the real aluminum $P_0$ must be derived or measured; it must not be chosen merely to manufacture fatigue behavior.

## 10. Decision rule

Promote this candidate toward the active theory only if all of the following hold:

1. a physically defensible construction of aluminum $P_0$ is obtained;
2. the effective local inertia and stress coupling are derived consistently;
3. the candidate reproduces relevant higher-fidelity or experimental $P$, tail, and first-passage trends over the regime of interest;
4. its errors are materially smaller than the uncertainty of the intended fatigue prediction.

If these conditions fail, return to the exact finite-chain / correlation-hierarchy checkpoint rather than adding arbitrary diffusion, damping, or a named PDF closure.
