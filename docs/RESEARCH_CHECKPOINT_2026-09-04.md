## 3. Microscopic dynamics and mechanically generated probability

Use physical spacing `a` as the paper-level probability coordinate and

$$
\lambda=\frac{a}{a_0}
$$

as the normalized computational coordinate.

The normalized generalized-LJ potential is

$$
\phi(\lambda)
=\frac{\lambda^{-m}}{m(m-n)}
-\frac{\lambda^{-n}}{n(m-n)}.
$$

Its derivatives are

$$
\phi'(\lambda)
=\frac{\lambda^{-n-1}-\lambda^{-m-1}}{m-n},
$$

$$
\phi''(\lambda)
=\frac{(m+1)\lambda^{-m-2}-(n+1)\lambda^{-n-2}}{m-n}.
$$

Current numerical exponents:

$$
m=12.19,\qquad n=6.
$$

The external-force bridge currently used for calibration is

$$
q(t)=\frac{\sigma_n(t)}{E}.
$$

This is a calibration definition, not a universal atomistic identity.

For one deterministic finite chain, the exact empirical spacing measure is

$$
P_{a,M}(a,t)
=\frac1M\sum_{i=1}^{M}\delta[a-a_i(t)].
$$

Hence no named PDF family is assumed. The probability state is generated by microscopic trajectories.

The current central map is

$$
\sigma_{\rm ext}(0:t),\Gamma_0
\longrightarrow

P_a(a,t).

## 4. Meaning of the initial full-state measure `mu_0`

The complete initial microscopic state is

$$
\Gamma_0=(x_1,\ldots,x_M,\dot x_1,\ldots,\dot x_M).
$$

`mu_0` is the probability measure over possible initial full microscopic states:

$$
\int \mu_0(d\Gamma_0)=1.
$$

If the initial microscopic state is completely deterministic,

$$
\mu_0=\delta_{\Gamma_0^*}.
$$

For a general initial-state ensemble, the exact push-forward spacing distribution is

$$
P_a(a,t)
=\frac1M\sum_i\int
\delta[a-A_i(t;\Gamma_0,\sigma)]
\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
$$

Important information hierarchy:

$$
\mu_0\longrightarrow P_a(a,0)
$$

is possible, but in general

$$
P_a(a,0)\not\longrightarrow \mu_0.
$$

The initial marginal `P_a(a,0)` discards initial velocities and spatial/neighbor correlations.

Therefore the present exact theory supports

$$
\mu_0+\sigma(t)\Longrightarrow P_a(a,t),
$$

but **does not yet support the general autonomous map**

$$
P_a(a,0)+\sigma(t)\Longrightarrow P_a(a,t)
$$

without additional initial information or closure.

For the perfectly specified ideal state, where all spacings and velocities are fixed, the deterministic `Gamma_0` is already known and the stress history is sufficient to compute the later `P_a`.

## 5. Phase-space probability and exact reduced fields

The empirical phase-space measure is

$$
F_M(\lambda,c,\tau)
=\frac1M\sum_i
\delta[\lambda-\lambda_i(\tau)]
\delta[c-c_i(\tau)].
$$

The spacing marginal is

$$
P_\lambda(\lambda,\tau)=\int F(\lambda,c,\tau)\,dc.
$$

Define

$$
u(\lambda,\tau)=\mathrm{E}[c\mid\lambda,\tau],
$$

$$
\Theta(\lambda,\tau)
=\mathrm{Var}(c\mid\lambda,\tau).
$$

The exact continuity equation is

$$
\partial_\tau P_\lambda
+\partial_\lambda(P_\lambda u)=0.
$$

The exact mean-rate equation is

$$
D_\tau u
=\mathcal A
-\frac1{P_\lambda}\partial_\lambda(P_\lambda\Theta),
$$

where

$$
\mathcal A
=\mathrm{E}[\ddot\lambda_i\mid\lambda_i=\lambda,\tau].
$$

The `Theta` equation still contains higher conditional information through `C_3` and `Psi`. Therefore `(P,u,Theta)` is a history-bearing reduced descriptor, not yet a closed autonomous Markov state.

## 6. Exact density-shape relation

Where `Theta` is nonzero,

$$
\Theta\,\partial_\lambda\ln P_\lambda
=\mathcal A-D_\tau u-\partial_\lambda\Theta.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
P_\lambda(\lambda,\tau)
=\frac{N_P(\tau)}{\Theta(\lambda,\tau)}
\exp\left[
\int_{\lambda_*}^{\lambda}
\frac{\mathcal A(\eta,\tau)-D_\tau u(\eta,\tau)}
{\Theta(\eta,\tau)}
\,d\eta
\right].
$$

This is not an assumed probability family. It is an exact smooth identity conditioned on the exact reduced fields. When `Theta` approaches zero, use the undivided transport equations instead.

Physical and normalized densities obey

$$
P_a(a,t)\,da=P_\lambda(\lambda,t)\,d\lambda,
$$

$$
P_\lambda(\lambda,t)=a_0P_a(a_0\lambda,t).
$$

## 8. Snapshot tail versus cumulative first passage

For an arbitrary threshold `lambda_*`, define the instantaneous tail

$$
Q_*(\tau)
=\int_{\lambda_*}^{\infty}
P_\lambda(\lambda,\tau)\,d\lambda.
$$

From continuity,

$$
\frac{dQ_*}{d\tau}=J(\lambda_*,\tau),
$$

where

$$
J=P_\lambda u.
$$

Over one cycle,

$$
Q_{*,n+1}-Q_{*,n}
=\int_{\tau_n}^{\tau_{n+1}}
J(\lambda_*,\tau)\,d\tau.
$$

Important correction established numerically: **snapshot tail mass is not generally monotone.** It can decrease through return crossings.

The cumulative first-passage fraction is

$$
F_*(\tau;\lambda_*)
=\frac1M\sum_i
I[\tau_i^*(\lambda_*)\le\tau],
$$

with

$$
\tau_i^*(\lambda_*)
=\inf\{\tau:\lambda_i(\tau)\ge\lambda_*\}.
$$

`F_*` is nondecreasing by construction and records threshold-crossing history.

This first-passage memory can exist in a conservative driven system and is distinct from G3 irreversible dissipation.

## 10. Registry coordinate `s` as future plasticity extension

The registry extension uses an intrinsic potential `U_0(a,s)` and defines the registry barrier

$$
\Delta G_s(a)
=U_0(a,s^\dagger)-U_0(a,s_0).
$$

Historical normalized direct-sum diagnostics gave strong barrier reduction with normal opening. In the retained diagnostic case,

- `Delta G_s` approximately `1.48754` near `a = 0.99196`
- `Delta G_s` approximately `0.13312` near the normal-curvature-loss point `a = 1.13069`

and the relevant branch satisfies

$$
\frac{d\Delta G_s}{da}<0.
$$

Therefore a high-`a` population maps mechanically to a low-registry-barrier population.

If

$$
g=\Delta G_s(a),
$$

then the barrier density is the push-forward

$$
P_G(g,t)
=\int P_a(a,t)
\delta[g-\Delta G_s(a)]\,da.
$$

On a monotone branch,

$$
P_G(g,t)
=\frac{P_a(a(g),t)}{|d\Delta G_s/da|_{a(g)}}.
$$

However, perfect symmetry remains invariant. If

$$
\partial_sU_0(a,s_0)=0,
$$

and

$$
s(0)=s_0,\qquad \dot s(0)=0,
$$

then the conservative registry equation admits

$$
s(t)=s_0.
$$

Thus barrier lowering alone does not create slip. A physically justified symmetry-breaking seed or mechanism is required.

Permanent plasticity is not established by barrier access or a transient crossing alone. Residual well-index change after unloading/relaxation is required.
