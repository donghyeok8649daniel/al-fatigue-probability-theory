# Milestone 21 — Deterministic spatial origin of the reduced state density

## 0. Question

The exact one-point density framework requires a represented set of states

$$
(a_\alpha,s_\alpha,\dot a_\alpha,\dot s_\alpha).
$$

If all represented states start identically and obey the same deterministic
ordinary differential equation, the empirical density remains a delta and
$\boldsymbol\Theta=0$.  Therefore the reduced model needs a *physical source of
state diversity* before a broad $P(a,s,t)$ can be interpreted.

This milestone identifies the minimal source already present in the original
1D layer problem: **spatial nonuniformity generated by boundary loading and
wave propagation**.  No random initial spread, Gaussian noise, Boltzmann law,
Fokker--Planck law, or empirical damage variable is introduced.

---

## 1. What the original 1D chain already proves

The original conservative chain uses atom positions $x_j$ and nearest-neighbor
spacings

$$
\lambda_i=x_{i+1}-x_i.
$$

With the left atom fixed and cyclic force applied only to the right atom,
interior spacings obey

$$
\boxed{
\ddot\lambda_i
=\phi'(\lambda_{i+1})-2\phi'(\lambda_i)+\phi'(\lambda_{i-1}).
}
$$

Even if

$$
\lambda_i(0)=1,\qquad \dot\lambda_i(0)=0
$$

for every $i$, the driven boundary breaks spatial uniformity.  The disturbance
propagates through the chain, so at finite time the set

$$
\{\lambda_i(t)\}
$$

is generally non-degenerate.  The finite-$M$ empirical density

$$
P_M(\lambda,t)=\frac1M\sum_i\delta[\lambda-\lambda_i(t)]
$$

therefore broadens **deterministically**.  The broadening does not require
stochastic forcing.

This is the source that should be retained when the active state is enlarged
from $a$ to $(a,s)$.

---

## 2. Spatial cells as the represented states

Take $M$ serial reduced cells and $M+1$ axial nodes.  Define

$$
\boxed{a_i=x_{i+1}-x_i},\qquad i=0,\ldots,M-1,
$$

and attach one unwrapped internal registry coordinate $s_i$ to each cell.

The represented-state index is therefore no longer an abstract independent
sample:

$$
\boxed{\alpha=i=\text{spatial cell index}.}
$$

The empirical two-coordinate measure is

$$
\boxed{
P_M(a,s,t)
=\frac1M\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}
\delta[a-a_i(t)]\delta[s-s_i(t)].
}
$$

This is a normalized one-point **spatial empirical measure**.  No independence
between cells is assumed or expected.

Its empirical currents are

$$
J_{a,M}
=\frac1M\sum_i\dot a_i
\delta[a-a_i]\delta[s-s_i],
$$

$$
J_{s,M}
=\frac1M\sum_i\dot s_i
\delta[a-a_i]\delta[s-s_i],
$$

and therefore

$$
\boxed{
\partial_tP_M+\partial_aJ_{a,M}+\partial_sJ_{s,M}=0
}
$$

holds distributionally without any probability closure.

---

## 3. Minimal local-state spatial energy: explicit new assumption

The active intrinsic energy is the local reduced state function

$$
U_0(a,s)
=\sum_{k\ge1}\sum_{p\in\mathbb Z}
v_{m,n}\!\left(\sqrt{k^2a^2+(pb+s)^2}\right).
$$

To let different spatial cells carry different values of $(a,s)$, one extra
assumption is unavoidable.  The minimal choice is the **local-state additive
extension**

$$
\boxed{
V_M(\mathbf x,\mathbf s)
=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}U_0(a_i,s_i),
\qquad a_i=x_{i+1}-x_i.
}
$$

Classification: **REDUCED-MODEL ASSUMPTION / LOCAL HOMOGENEITY EXTENSION.**

It is not claimed to be the exact nonuniform many-body energy.  Its purpose is
limited and transparent:

1. a spatially uniform state has intrinsic energy $U_0(a,s)$ per cell;
2. no artificial nearest-neighbor spring is added on top of $U_0$;
3. the axial node coupling follows automatically because adjacent cells share
   a node $x_i$;
4. local normal--registry coupling is exactly the already active mixed
   dependence of the same $U_0(a,s)$.

If this local-additivity assumption later fails numerical/experimental tests,
it must be replaced rather than hidden inside a fitted PDF.

---

## 4. Conservative equations generated by the local additive energy

Let every moving axial node have mass $m_x>0$.  Let every internal registry
coordinate have inertia $\mu_s>0$.  The latter is still a reduced parameter and
is **not** claimed to be fixed by $U_0$ itself.

The kinetic energy is

$$
T
=\frac12m_x\sum_{j=1}^{M}\dot x_j^2
+\frac12\mu_s\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\dot s_i^2,
$$

with $x_0$ fixed.

Let $Q_a(t)$ be the external generalized normal force on the right node and
$q_{s,i}(t)$ the declared generalized force conjugate to $s_i$.

For interior axial nodes,

$$
\boxed{
m_x\ddot x_j
=U_a(a_j,s_j)-U_a(a_{j-1},s_{j-1}),
}
$$

where

$$
U_a=\partial_aU_0.
$$

At the right boundary,

$$
\boxed{
m_x\ddot x_M
=-U_a(a_{M-1},s_{M-1})+Q_a(t).
}
$$

The registry equations are

$$
\boxed{
\mu_s\ddot s_i
=q_{s,i}(t)-U_s(a_i,s_i),
\qquad U_s=\partial_sU_0.
}
$$

No damping term and no random term has been added.

For interior spacings,

$$
\boxed{
m_x\ddot a_i
=U_a(a_{i+1},s_{i+1})
-2U_a(a_i,s_i)
+U_a(a_{i-1},s_{i-1}).
}
$$

This is the direct two-state analogue of the original nonlinear 1D spacing
Laplacian, but with the important caveat that it follows from the newly declared
local-additive $V_M$, not from a claim of an exact nonuniform many-body lattice
sum.

---

## 5. Deterministic mechanism that broadens $P(a,s,t)$

Start from a perfectly homogeneous deterministic state

$$
a_i(0)=a_0,\qquad s_i(0)=s_0,
$$

$$
\dot a_i(0)=\dot s_i(0)=0.
$$

At $t=0$, therefore,

$$
P_M(a,s,0)=\delta(a-a_0)\delta(s-s_0).
$$

A time-dependent force applied at the right boundary changes the last node
first.  Hence the last spacing no longer follows the same trajectory as the
interior spacings.  The shared-node equations then transmit that difference
into the interior.

Thus

$$
\boxed{
\text{boundary drive}
\rightarrow\text{spatially nonuniform }a_i(t).
}
$$

Because $U_s$ depends on both $a$ and $s$, cells with different $a_i$ generally
have different registry acceleration even when they have the same instantaneous
$q_s$:

$$
\ddot s_i-\ddot s_j
=-\frac{U_s(a_i,s_i)-U_s(a_j,s_j)}{\mu_s}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\text{nonuniform }a_i(t)
\rightarrow\text{nonuniform }s_i(t)
}
$$

whenever the mixed normal--registry coupling is active.

The joint density can consequently broaden from a delta **without any random
forcing**.

---

## 6. Linearized diversity-transfer equation

Around a spatially uniform reference trajectory $(\bar a(t),\bar s(t))$, write

$$
a_i=\bar a+\delta a_i,
\qquad
s_i=\bar s+\delta s_i.
$$

Define local Hessian entries

$$
K_{aa}=U_{aa}(\bar a,\bar s),
\qquad
K_{as}=U_{as}(\bar a,\bar s),
\qquad
K_{ss}=U_{ss}(\bar a,\bar s).
$$

For interior cells,

$$
\boxed{
m_x\,\delta\ddot a_i
=K_{aa}\Delta_d\delta a_i
+K_{as}\Delta_d\delta s_i,
}
$$

where

$$
\Delta_d y_i=y_{i+1}-2y_i+y_{i-1}.
$$

For a spatially uniform registry drive, $\delta q_{s,i}=0$,

$$
\boxed{
\mu_s\,\delta\ddot s_i
=-K_{as}\delta a_i-K_{ss}\delta s_i.
}
$$

The mixed curvature

$$
\boxed{K_{as}=\partial_{as}^2U_0}
$$

is therefore the deterministic transfer coefficient between normal spatial
heterogeneity and registry spatial heterogeneity.

This gives a direct physical route to a nonzero velocity cross covariance

$$
\Theta_{as}
=\operatorname{Cov}(\dot a,\dot s\mid a,s)
$$

without assuming correlated noise.

### Symmetry caveat

For the present cosine-periodic registry energy, a symmetric registry minimum
such as $s=0$ satisfies

$$
U_s(a,0)=0
$$

for every $a$, and therefore

$$
\boxed{U_{as}(a,0)=0.}
$$

Hence pure normal heterogeneity does **not** linearly generate registry spread
while the system remains exactly at the symmetric registry minimum.  The
normal-to-registry diversity transfer activates after registry is displaced
from that symmetry point by a declared registry drive or by another physical
mechanism.

This is a useful falsifiable prediction of the current reduced energy rather
than a parameter inserted by hand.

## 7. Energy balance

For the conservative spatial model,

$$
E_M
=T+\sum_iU_0(a_i,s_i).
$$

The exact work balance is

$$
\boxed{
\frac{dE_M}{dt}
=Q_a(t)\dot x_M
+\sum_i q_{s,i}(t)\dot s_i.
}
$$

Thus boundary-induced spreading of the empirical density does not itself imply
irreversible dissipation.  With no dissipative mechanism, G3 must still not be
manufactured from this model.

G2, however, is immediate as the empirical one-point mean intrinsic energy,

$$
\boxed{
\bar U_M(t)
=\frac1M\sum_i
\left[U_0(a_i,s_i)-U_0(a_0,s_0)\right]
}
$$

which is exactly the finite-cell quadrature counterpart of

$$
\bar U=\iint\Delta U_0(a,s)P(a,s,t)\,da\,ds.
$$

## 8. Relation to the exact $\Theta$ shape identity

From the deterministic cell states define

$$
\mathbf v_i=(\dot a_i,\dot s_i).
$$

The conditional fields

$$
\mathbf u(a,s,t),
\qquad
\boldsymbol\Theta(a,s,t),
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\mathcal A}(a,s,t)
$$

can then be estimated from the empirical state cloud without assuming a named
PDF family.

The reconstructed smooth density must satisfy

$$
\boxed{
\boldsymbol\Theta\nabla\ln P
=\boldsymbol{\mathcal A}-D_t\mathbf u-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\Theta.
}
$$

This is now used correctly as a **mechanics-consistency/reconstruction test**,
not as an independent closed PDE.

If the state cloud remains on a lower-dimensional curve or if the local
conditional velocity covariance is singular, the divided
$\boldsymbol\Theta^{-1}$ formula must not be used.

## 9. Probability interpretation: an important scale distinction

The measure constructed above is initially

$$
P_M(a,s,t)
=\frac1M\sum_i\delta(a-a_i)\delta(s-s_i),
$$

so it is the distribution of the state of a **uniformly sampled spatial cell**
in one deterministic specimen/model realization.

Therefore

$$
S_{\rm cell}(t)
=\iint_{\Omega_b}P(a,s,t)\,da\,ds
$$

is immediately interpretable as the intact fraction/probability for a randomly
sampled represented cell.

It is **not automatically** the survival probability of the entire specimen.
For specimen survival, the event is closer to

$$
\{\text{all crack-relevant cells remain in }\Omega_b\},
$$

and its probability depends on spatial correlations and on what ensemble of
nominally identical specimens/microstates is intended.

Consequently the identification

$$
P_{\rm crack,specimen}=1-S_{\rm cell}
$$

is not allowed without an additional statistical-scale/ergodic argument.
This is consistent with the earlier warning that FEM elements or axial cells
must not be assumed statistically independent before an event-relevant
correlation scale converges.

The present deterministic spatial construction **does** provide G1, G2, local
state tails, and a physically generated one-point density.  The specimen-level
G4 probability interpretation remains a separate scale-bridging problem.

## 10. What has been gained

The reduced theory can now generate a nontrivial state cloud without arbitrary
noise through the chain

$$
\boxed{
\text{cyclic boundary force}
\rightarrow
\text{spatial }a_i\text{ wave/gradient}
\rightarrow
U_{as}\text{ coupling}
\rightarrow
\text{spatial }s_i\text{ diversity}
\rightarrow
P_M(a,s,t).
}
$$

The new ingredient is not a fitted probability law.  It is a declared spatial
replication of the active local energy and deterministic boundary-value
mechanics.

## 11. Assumption ledger after this milestone

### Exact/kinematic within the declared finite cell state

- definition of $a_i=x_{i+1}-x_i$;
- empirical $P_M$, currents, and continuity;
- Euler--Lagrange equations once $V_M$, inertias, and external generalized
  forces are declared;
- conservative energy/work identity;
- moment/Theta reconstruction identities.

### New reduced-model assumption

- local-state additivity $V_M=\sum_iU_0(a_i,s_i)$.

### Still unresolved physical quantities/closures

- quantitative node mass and registry inertia paired with the reduced energy;
- physical mapping of tensile loading to $Q_a$ and internal registry drive;
- irreversible mechanism for G3 and permanent slip;
- conversion of the spatial one-point survival fraction to specimen-level
  crack-initiation probability;
- validation that the local-additive nonuniform extension preserves the
  crack-relevant behavior of the intended material.

No FCC model is introduced or required by this milestone.
