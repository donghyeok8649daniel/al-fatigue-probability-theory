# Pure-normal cycle accumulation no-go and external literature check

Status: **ACTIVE LIMITATION / final consistency check for the reduced 1D mainline.**

This note separates a successful mathematical closure from a physically successful fatigue mechanism. The present 1D normal model can be closed from a structural initial spacing density and a prescribed stress history, but under the strict quasistatic/fast-equilibration assumptions its cycle-frequency scaling is highly constrained. That constraint can be compared directly with published aluminum fatigue observations.

## 1. Assumptions of the strict pure-normal reduced model

Assume:

1. the structural initial state is described by $P_0(\lambda)$;
2. each intact structural label follows the quasistatic stable branch

$$
\phi'[\Lambda(\lambda_0,t)]
=\phi'(\lambda_0)+q(t)-q_{\mathrm{ref}};
$$

3. a closed stress cycle returns each intact mechanical label to its reference-phase spacing if the stable branch is not lost;
4. there is no additional slow cycle-evolving internal state;
5. any renewed first-passage opportunity is generated by a fast stationary thermal bath with an instantaneous escape rate $k_c[\Lambda(t),T]$;
6. the loading waveform has the same phase shape at different frequencies.

These assumptions cover the currently active quasistatic normal transport plus fast-equilibration thermal first-passage closure.

## 2. Deterministic pure-normal first passage cannot accumulate indefinitely

If the thermal sink is removed and all structural labels are exactly periodic,

$$
\Lambda(\lambda_0,t+T_f)=\Lambda(\lambda_0,t),
$$

then the set of labels that deterministically cross a fixed threshold is already determined in the first completed cycle. Repeating the identical cycle exposes the same labels again. Therefore deterministic cumulative first passage saturates after the first cycle:

$$
F_{\mathrm{det}}(N\ge1)=F_{\mathrm{det}}(1).
$$

Thus a label-preserving conservative/quasistatic normal model cannot by itself generate high-cycle accumulation.

## 3. Fast-equilibrium thermal renewal is elapsed-time controlled

For a periodic waveform write phase $\theta=ft$ with $0\le\theta<1$. In the quasistatic limit the stable branch is a function of phase, not frequency:

$$
\Lambda_f(t)=\Lambda_*(\theta).
$$

The integrated one-cycle hazard is

$$
\mathcal H_f
=\int_0^{1/f}k_c[\Lambda_f(t),T]dt.
$$

Using $dt=d\theta/f$ gives the exact scaling

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal H_f
=\frac1f
\int_0^1k_c[\Lambda_*(\theta),T]d\theta.
}
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{\mathcal H_f\propto f^{-1}.}
$$

For a narrow structural population in the rare-event regime,

$$
S_N\simeq\exp(-N\mathcal H_f).
$$

Therefore median **time** to first passage is approximately independent of frequency, whereas median **cycles** scale linearly with frequency:

$$
\boxed{N_{50}\propto f.}
$$

This is not a fitted prediction. It is a direct consequence of the strict quasistatic/fast-equilibration assumptions.

## 4. Quantitative frequency-ratio test

A classic high-purity aluminum study reported room-temperature fatigue strength to be insensitive to frequency over 25--1440 cycles/min. The corresponding frequencies are

$$
f_{\min}=\frac{25}{60}\approx0.4167\ \mathrm{Hz},
\qquad
f_{\max}=\frac{1440}{60}=24\ \mathrm{Hz}.
$$

The strict reduced thermal model predicts

$$
\frac{\mathcal H(f_{\min})}{\mathcal H(f_{\max})}
=\frac{f_{\max}}{f_{\min}}
=57.6.
$$

Thus, at the same stress waveform and same number of cycles, the low-frequency test would accumulate about 57.6 times the local hazard of the high-frequency test under the strict model. A broad room-temperature frequency insensitivity in fatigue strength is therefore not naturally compatible with a dominant fast-equilibrium pure-normal thermal escape mechanism.

The historical frequency study is not an exact match to the present single-crystal target, so it is a falsification warning rather than a complete rejection by itself. However, the magnitude and sign of the model prediction are strong enough that frequency testing must be an early experiment.

## 5. Single-crystal aluminum literature points to cycle-evolving slip/dislocation structure

Published aluminum single-crystal observations provide an additional mechanism check.

- T. Zhai, G. A. D. Briggs, and J. W. Martin, *Acta Materialia* 44(9), 3489--3496 (1996), DOI `10.1016/1359-6454(96)00025-0`, studied Al single crystals under push-pull loading at room temperature and 20 Hz. Secondary slip lines, persistent-slip-band-related extrusions/intrusions, and later short cracks were observed; the authors associated crack initiation with internal stress generated by net irreversible secondary slip.
- M. Hayashi, *International Journal of Fatigue* 156, 106661 (2022), DOI `10.1016/j.ijfatigue.2021.106661`, studied 99.99% pure aluminum single crystals. Crack-initiation life depended strongly on crystal orientation and multiple slip systems, and subgrain misorientation at initiation suggested a dislocation-density-related structural criterion.
- N. H. G. Daniels and J. E. Dorn, “The Effect of Temperature, Frequency, and Grain Size on the Fatigue Properties of High-Purity Aluminum,” ASTM STP 196, p. 94 (1957), DOI `10.1520/STP19619570007`, reported room-temperature fatigue strength insensitive to frequency over 25--1440 cycles/min for high-purity aluminum.

These observations do not prove that a normal-spacing submodel is useless. They do show that real room-temperature aluminum fatigue has a major cycle-evolving slip/dislocation component that the current pure-normal state does not contain.

## 6. Final no-go statement under the strict current constraints

Within the simultaneous assumptions

$$
\boxed{
\text{pure normal coordinate}
+\text{quasistatic reversible mechanics}
+\text{no slow internal state}
}
$$

there are only two available accumulation routes considered so far:

1. deterministic threshold crossing, which saturates after repeated identical cycles; or
2. fast-equilibrium thermal crossing, which gives $\mathcal H_{\mathrm{cycle}}\propto1/f$ and therefore an elapsed-time-controlled life.

Consequently:

$$
\boxed{
\text{a genuinely cycle-controlled high-cycle fatigue law cannot be obtained from the strict pure-normal }P_0\text{-only state without additional cycle-evolving physics.}
}
$$

This is the final structural limitation discovered by the closure search.

## 7. What remains successful

The following result remains valid and useful as a **normal-instability submodel**:

$$
P_0+\sigma(0:t)
\longrightarrow
P_b(\lambda,t),\ S(t),\ F_{\mathrm{ci}}(t)
$$

under the explicitly declared quasistatic and transition-state assumptions. It is mathematically closed, contains no named fatigue-life distribution, and is experimentally falsifiable.

What is no longer justified is calling that closure a complete physical theory of room-temperature pure-aluminum fatigue before the frequency and slip/dislocation evidence is reconciled.

## 8. Minimal physics required for a full material model

To recover genuinely cycle-controlled fatigue while preserving the original requirement that the calculation starts from $P_0$ and the load history, at least one additional **physical** internal state must evolve during cycling. Its initial state may be fixed by the reference preparation, so the user-level input can still remain $P_0+\sigma(0:t)$, but the intermediate constitutive state cannot in general remain $P$ alone.

The missing state must have all of the following properties:

1. it changes irreversibly or metastably under cycling;
2. its change is tied to loading reversals/cyclic slip rather than merely elapsed time;
3. it feeds back on the normal-spacing distribution or normal first-passage barrier;
4. it is derived from mechanics, not inserted as an empirical scalar damage variable;
5. it admits a fixed or independently measurable initial condition.

For real aluminum, the literature points to slip/dislocation or subgrain structure as the physically likely class. If the project keeps the strict no-shear/no-slip restriction, then the scientifically correct scope is a **1D normal-instability first-passage submodel**, not a complete pure-Al fatigue theory.

## 9. Final research conclusion

The closure search has reached a definitive endpoint:

- exact microscopic projection: mathematically exact but not autonomous in one-point $P_0$;
- pure-normal lab dynamics: quasistatic and reversible;
- permanent normalized-$P$ drift: not mathematically necessary;
- fast thermal first passage: provides a closed survivor law but predicts elapsed-time-controlled accumulation;
- registry-kink storage in the present ideal model: not established as a long-lived cycle memory;
- external aluminum evidence: strongly motivates a cycle-evolving slip/dislocation state for a full material model.

Therefore the current work has **successfully closed the normal-instability subproblem and successfully proved the limitation of a strict pure-normal fatigue theory**. The next scientific decision is binary:

1. retain the paper as a normal-instability submodel and validate/falsify $A_c$, $P_0$, $\lambda_c$, transmission, temperature, and frequency; or
2. expand the state with a physically derived cycle-evolving slip/dislocation variable if the objective remains a complete room-temperature pure-aluminum fatigue theory.
