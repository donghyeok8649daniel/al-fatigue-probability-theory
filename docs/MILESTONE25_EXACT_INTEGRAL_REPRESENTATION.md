# Milestone 25 — exact integral representation of the active 1D state

## 0. Purpose

The active one-point equations are exact but hierarchical. That does **not** mean that the reduced state lacks an exact mathematical solution representation.

Two distinct statements must be separated:

1. an autonomous PDE involving only $P,u,\Theta$ is not closed without higher conditional statistics;
2. given the closed finite LJ chain and an initial full-state measure, the projected quantities $F,P,u,\Theta$ have exact push-forward/integral representations.

No Boltzmann, Fokker--Planck, Gaussian/Weibull law, Markov closure, damping law, or empirical damage variable is introduced.

All symbols in this file follow `VARIABLE_INDEX_1D_P_U_THETA.md`.

---

## 1. Closed finite-chain flow

Let

$$
\boxed{
\Gamma=(x_1,\ldots,x_M,\dot x_1,\ldots,\dot x_M)
}
$$

be the full finite-chain state required by the deterministic normal LJ dynamics.
For prescribed loading $q(\tau)$, define the deterministic flow

$$
\boxed{
\Gamma(\tau)=\Phi_{\tau,\tau_0}^{q}(\Gamma_0).
}
$$

For each represented spacing $i$,

$$
\boxed{
\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)
=x_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)-x_{i-1}(\tau;\Gamma_0)
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
C_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)=\frac{d\Lambda_i}{d\tau},
\qquad
A_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)=\frac{d^2\Lambda_i}{d\tau^2}.
}
$$

The functions $\Lambda_i,C_i,A_i$ are generated by the same finite LJ equations already fixed in the active mainline.

**Status:** EXACT under the adopted finite LJ model and loading/boundary law.

## 2. Initial full-state measure

Let $\mu_0(d\Gamma_0)$ be any normalized measure on admissible initial finite-chain states,

$$
\boxed{
\int\mu_0(d\Gamma_0)=1.
}
$$

This notation does not impose a thermal or equilibrium ensemble.

Two important cases are:

1. one deterministic chain,
   $$
   \boxed{\mu_0=\delta_{\Gamma_0^*}},
   $$
   where one-point probability comes only from uniform spatial counting over spacing index;
2. an ensemble of microscopic/specimen realizations, where $\mu_0$ also represents physically justified initial-state variability and correlation.

The formulas below are valid in both cases. The physical choice of $\mu_0$ for specimen-to-specimen fatigue remains a modeling/calibration problem.

## 3. Exact push-forward integral for the phase-space state

$$
\boxed{
F(\lambda,c,\tau)
=\frac1M\sum_{i=1}^{M}
\int
\delta[\lambda-\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)]
\delta[c-C_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

This is the exact push-forward of the initial full-state measure through the closed deterministic chain, followed by uniform one-point spatial sampling.

If $\mu_0=\delta_{\Gamma_0^*}$,

$$
\boxed{
F_M(\lambda,c,\tau)
=\frac1M\sum_i
\delta(\lambda-\lambda_i)\delta(c-c_i).
}
$$

Therefore the reduced PDE hierarchy is **not required to define or compute $F$** if the full finite chain is evolved directly.

## 4. Exact integral formulas for $P,u,\Theta$

Integrating over $c$ gives

$$
\boxed{
P(\lambda,\tau)
=\frac1M\sum_i
\int
\delta[\lambda-\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

The first spacing-rate moment is

$$
\boxed{
P(\lambda,\tau)u(\lambda,\tau)
=\frac1M\sum_i
\int
C_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)
\delta[\lambda-\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

The second raw spacing-rate moment is

$$
\boxed{
P(u^2+\Theta)
=\frac1M\sum_i
\int
C_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)^2
\delta[\lambda-\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

Hence, wherever $P>0$,

$$
\boxed{
 u(\lambda,\tau)
=\frac{
\frac1M\sum_i\int C_i\,\delta(\lambda-\Lambda_i)\,d\mu_0
}{P(\lambda,\tau)}
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\Theta(\lambda,\tau)
=\frac{
\frac1M\sum_i\int C_i^2\,\delta(\lambda-\Lambda_i)\,d\mu_0
}{P(\lambda,\tau)}
-u(\lambda,\tau)^2.
}
$$

Thus $P,u,\Theta$ possess exact integral solution representations in terms of the deterministic LJ flow.

## 5. Exact integral formulas for hierarchy sources

The one-point conditional acceleration satisfies

$$
\boxed{
P\mathcal A
=\frac1M\sum_i
\int
A_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)
\delta[\lambda-\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

The third conditional central moment satisfies

$$
\boxed{
PC_3
=\frac1M\sum_i
\int
[C_i-u(\lambda,\tau)]^3
\delta[\lambda-\Lambda_i]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

The acceleration-covariance source satisfies

$$
\boxed{
P\Psi
=\frac1M\sum_i
\int
[C_i-u(\lambda,\tau)]A_i
\delta[\lambda-\Lambda_i]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

Therefore $C_3$ and $\Psi$ are not arbitrary closure coefficients. They also have exact projection integrals over the same deterministic trajectories.

The reduced hierarchy is a loss-of-information issue, not a lack-of-definition issue.

## 6. Exact Volterra time-integral form

The continuity equation integrates to

$$
\boxed{
P(\lambda,\tau)
=P_0(\lambda)
-\partial_\lambda
\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
P(\lambda,s)u(\lambda,s)\,ds.
}
$$

The first-moment equation gives

$$
\boxed{
Pu
=P_0u_0
-\partial_\lambda
\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
P(u^2+\Theta)(\lambda,s)\,ds
+\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
P\mathcal A(\lambda,s)\,ds.
}
$$

The second raw moment gives

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
P(u^2+\Theta)(\lambda,\tau)
={}&P_0(u_0^2+\Theta_0)(\lambda)\\
&-\partial_\lambda\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
P(u^3+3u\Theta+C_3)(\lambda,s)\,ds\\
&+2\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
P[u\mathcal A+\Psi](\lambda,s)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

These are exact Volterra-type integral equations. They remain hierarchical, but every higher source can be evaluated by the full-flow projection formulas above.

## 7. Characteristic integral form for $P$

For smooth spacing-space mean velocity $u$, define

$$
\boxed{
\frac{dX}{ds}=u(X(s),s),
\qquad
X(\tau_0)=\alpha.
}
$$

Define the accumulated mean-flow gradient

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal I_u(s;\alpha)
=\int_{\tau_0}^{s}
\partial_\lambda u(X(r),r)\,dr.
}
$$

Along the characteristic,

$$
\boxed{
P(X(\tau),\tau)
=P_0(\alpha)
\exp[-\mathcal I_u(\tau;\alpha)].
}
$$

Equivalently, while the characteristic map is differentiable and one-to-one,

$$
\boxed{
P(X(\alpha,\tau),\tau)
\frac{\partial X}{\partial\alpha}
=P_0(\alpha).
}
$$

## 8. Characteristic integral form for $u$

Along the same spacing-space characteristic,

$$
\frac{du}{ds}
=\mathcal A
-\frac1P\partial_\lambda(P\Theta).
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
 u(X(\tau),\tau)
=u_0(\alpha)
+\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
\left[
\mathcal A
-\frac1P\partial_\lambda(P\Theta)
\right]_{(X(s),s)}ds.
}
$$

This equation is exact but implicit because the integrand is generated by the coupled reduced/full state.

## 9. Characteristic integral form for $\Theta$

The exact general equation is

$$
D_\tau\Theta
+2\Theta\partial_\lambda u
=2\Psi-\frac1P\partial_\lambda(PC_3).
$$

Define

$$
\boxed{
S_\Theta
=2\Psi-\frac1P\partial_\lambda(PC_3).
}
$$

Along $X(s)$,

$$
\frac{d\Theta}{ds}
+2(\partial_\lambda u)\Theta
=S_\Theta.
$$

Using the integrating factor $e^{2\mathcal I_u}$ gives

$$
\boxed{
\Theta(X(\tau),\tau)
=e^{-2\mathcal I_u(\tau;\alpha)}
\left[
\Theta_0(\alpha)
+\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
 e^{2\mathcal I_u(s;\alpha)}
 S_\Theta(X(s),s)\,ds
\right].
}
$$

Since

$$
\frac{P(X(s),s)}{P_0(\alpha)}=e^{-\mathcal I_u(s;\alpha)},
$$

an equivalent form is

$$
\boxed{
\Theta(X(\tau),\tau)
=\left[\frac{P(X(\tau),\tau)}{P_0(\alpha)}\right]^2
\left[
\Theta_0(\alpha)
+\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
\left(\frac{P_0(\alpha)}{P(X(s),s)}\right)^2
S_\Theta(X(s),s)\,ds
\right].
}
$$

Thus $\Theta$ has a complete exact integral form. The remaining issue is obtaining the mechanics-generated $C_3$ and $\Psi$, either through higher reduced moments or directly from finite LJ trajectories.

## 10. Projected phase-space characteristic representation

If the exact conditional acceleration field $A(\lambda,c,\tau)$ is known and sufficiently smooth, let a projected phase-space characteristic be denoted by $(\lambda_{\rm ph}(s),c_{\rm ph}(s))$ and satisfy

$$
\boxed{
\frac{d\lambda_{\rm ph}}{ds}=c_{\rm ph},
\qquad
\frac{dc_{\rm ph}}{ds}=A(\lambda_{\rm ph},c_{\rm ph},s).
}
$$

Along this characteristic,

$$
\frac{dF}{ds}=-F\,\partial_cA.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
F(\lambda_{\rm ph}(\tau),c_{\rm ph}(\tau),\tau)
=F_0(\lambda_{\rm ph}(\tau_0),c_{\rm ph}(\tau_0))
\exp\left[-\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
\partial_cA(\lambda_{\rm ph}(s),c_{\rm ph}(s),s)\,ds\right].
}
$$

This is exact for the projected kinetic equation once the true $A$ is supplied. For the present chain, the full-flow push-forward formula in Section 3 is more fundamental because it performs the projection without treating $A$ as an autonomous one-point constitutive law.

## 12. Exact path-integral form of local and specimen first passage

For one spacing trajectory,

$$
\boxed{
\tau_i^c(\Gamma_0)
=\inf\{\tau\ge\tau_0:\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)\ge\lambda_c\}.
}
$$

The local survivor fraction/ensemble probability is

$$
\boxed{
S_{\rm local}(\tau)
=\frac1M\sum_i
\int
\mathbf1\!\left[
\sup_{s\in[\tau_0,\tau]}
\Lambda_i(s;\Gamma_0)<\lambda_c
\right]
\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
F_{\rm ci}^{\rm local}(\tau)=1-S_{\rm local}(\tau).
}
$$

For a specimen realization, first initiation is the first failure among all represented spacings:

$$
\boxed{
S_{\rm spec}(\tau)
=\int
\mathbf1\!\left[
\max_{1\le i\le M}
\sup_{s\in[\tau_0,\tau]}
\Lambda_i(s;\Gamma_0)<\lambda_c
\right]
\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
F_{\rm ci}^{\rm spec}(\tau)=1-S_{\rm spec}(\tau).
}
$$

No product of independent atomic/cell/FEM probabilities is required. Spatial correlation is retained when present in the full-state flow and in $\mu_0$.

The unresolved specimen-scale problem is therefore

$$
\boxed{
\text{determine/validate the physical }\mu_0
\text{ and represented correlation scale.}
}
$$
