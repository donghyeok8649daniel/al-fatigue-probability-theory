# Milestone 25 — exact integral representation of the active 1D state

## 0. Purpose

The active one-point equations are exact but hierarchical. That does **not** mean
that the reduced state lacks an exact mathematical solution representation.
There are two distinct statements:

1. an autonomous PDE involving only $P,u,\Theta$ is not closed without higher
   conditional statistics;
2. given the closed finite LJ chain and an initial full-state measure, the
   projected quantities $F,P,u,\Theta$ have exact push-forward/integral
   representations.

This milestone records those integral forms. No Boltzmann, Fokker--Planck,
Gaussian/Weibull law, Markov closure, damping law, or empirical damage variable
is introduced.

---

## 1. Closed finite-chain flow

Let $\Gamma$ denote the full finite-chain state required by the deterministic
normal LJ dynamics, for example all moving-node positions and velocities,

$$
\Gamma=(x_1,\ldots,x_M,\dot x_1,\ldots,\dot x_M).
$$

For a prescribed loading history $q(\tau)$, write the deterministic flow as

$$
\boxed{
\Gamma(\tau)=\Phi_{\tau,\tau_0}^{q}(\Gamma_0).
}
$$

For each represented spacing $i$, define the projections of that flow

$$
\boxed{
\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)
= x_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)-x_{i-1}(\tau;\Gamma_0),
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
C_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)=\frac{d\Lambda_i}{d\tau},
\qquad
A_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)=\frac{d^2\Lambda_i}{d\tau^2}.
}
$$

The functions $\Lambda_i,C_i,A_i$ are generated by the same finite LJ equations
already fixed in the active mainline.

**Status:** EXACT under the adopted finite LJ model and loading/boundary law.

---

## 2. Initial full-state measure

Let $\mu_0(d\Gamma_0)$ be any normalized measure on the admitted initial
finite-chain states,

$$
\boxed{
\int \mu_0(d\Gamma_0)=1.
}
$$

This notation does not impose a thermal or equilibrium ensemble.

Two important cases are:

1. one deterministic chain,
   $$
   \mu_0=\delta_{\Gamma_0^*},
   $$
   in which the only probability in the one-point density is the uniform
   spatial counting over spacing index $i$;
2. an ensemble of microscopic/specimen realizations, in which $\mu_0$ also
   represents physically justified initial-state variability/correlation.

The mathematical formulas below are valid in both cases. The physical choice of
$\mu_0$ for specimen-to-specimen fatigue remains a separate modeling/calibration
problem.

---

## 3. Exact push-forward integral for the phase-space state

The projected spacing/rate density is

$$
\boxed{
F(\lambda,c,\tau)
=\frac1M\sum_{i=1}^{M}
\int
\delta[\lambda-\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)]
\delta[c-C_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

This is the exact push-forward of the initial full-state measure through the
closed deterministic chain, followed by uniform one-point spatial sampling.

If $\mu_0=\delta_{\Gamma_0^*}$, this reduces exactly to the empirical measure

$$
F_M(\lambda,c,\tau)
=\frac1M\sum_i
\delta(\lambda-\lambda_i)\delta(c-c_i).
$$

Therefore the hierarchy of reduced PDEs is **not required to define or compute
$F$** if the full finite chain is evolved directly.

## 4. Exact integral formulas for $P,u,\Theta$

Integrating over $c$ gives

$$
\boxed{
P(\lambda,\tau)
=\frac1M\sum_i
\int
\delta[\lambda-\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

The first spacing-rate moment is

$$
\boxed{
P(\lambda,\tau)u(\lambda,\tau)
=\frac1M\sum_i
\int
C_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)
\delta[\lambda-\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

The second raw spacing-rate moment is

$$
\boxed{
P(u^2+\Theta)
=\frac1M\sum_i
\int
C_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)^2
\delta[\lambda-\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

Hence, wherever $P>0$,

$$
\boxed{
u
=\frac{
\frac1M\sum_i\int C_i\,\delta(\lambda-\Lambda_i)\,d\mu_0
}{P}
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\Theta
=\frac{
\frac1M\sum_i\int C_i^2\,\delta(\lambda-\Lambda_i)\,d\mu_0
}{P}
-u^2.
}
$$

Thus $P,u,\Theta$ themselves possess exact integral solution representations in
terms of the deterministic LJ flow.

## 5. Exact integral formulas for the hierarchy sources

The one-point conditional acceleration satisfies

$$
\boxed{
P\mathcal A
=\frac1M\sum_i
\int
A_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)
\delta[\lambda-\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

The third conditional central moment satisfies

$$
\boxed{
PC_3
=\frac1M\sum_i
\int
[C_i-u(\lambda,\tau)]^3
\delta[\lambda-\Lambda_i]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

The acceleration-covariance source satisfies

$$
\boxed{
P\Psi
=\frac1M\sum_i
\int
[C_i-u(\lambda,\tau)]A_i
\delta[\lambda-\Lambda_i]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

Therefore $C_3$ and $\Psi$ are not arbitrary closure coefficients. They also
have exact projection integrals over the same deterministic trajectories.

The reduced hierarchy is a loss-of-information issue, not a lack-of-definition
issue.

## 6. Time-integrated Volterra form of the first moments

The continuity equation

$$
\partial_\tau P+\partial_\lambda(Pu)=0
$$

integrates exactly to

$$
\boxed{
P(\lambda,\tau)
=P_0(\lambda)
-\partial_\lambda
\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
P(\lambda,s)u(\lambda,s)\,ds.
}
$$

The first-moment equation gives

$$
\boxed{
P u
=P_0u_0
-\partial_\lambda
\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
P(u^2+\Theta)(\lambda,s)\,ds
+\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
P\mathcal A(\lambda,s)\,ds.
}
$$

The second raw moment gives

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
P(u^2+\Theta)(\lambda,\tau)
={}&P_0(u_0^2+\Theta_0)(\lambda)\\
&-\partial_\lambda\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
P(u^3+3u\Theta+C_3)(\lambda,s)\,ds\\
&+2\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
P[u\mathcal A+\Psi](\lambda,s)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

These are exact Volterra-type integral equations. They remain hierarchical, but
no term is undefined: every higher source can be evaluated by the full-flow
projection formulas in Sections 3--5.

## 7. Characteristic integral form for $P$

For a smooth spacing-space mean velocity $u$, define a characteristic

$$
\boxed{
\frac{dX}{ds}=u(X(s),s),
\qquad
X(\tau_0)=\alpha.
}
$$

Define

$$
\boxed{
I(s;\alpha)
=\int_{\tau_0}^{s}
\partial_\lambda u(X(r),r)\,dr.
}
$$

Along the characteristic, continuity gives

$$
\boxed{
P(X(\tau),\tau)
=P_0(\alpha)
\exp[-I(\tau;\alpha)].
}
$$

Equivalently, while the characteristic map remains differentiable and
one-to-one,

$$
\boxed{
P(X(\alpha,\tau),\tau)
\,\frac{\partial X}{\partial\alpha}
=P_0(\alpha).
}
$$

This is the exact transport/push-forward representation of the smooth one-point
density.

---

## 8. Characteristic integral form for $u$

Along the same spacing-space characteristic,

$$
\frac{du}{ds}
=\mathcal A
-\frac1P\partial_\lambda(P\Theta).
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
u(X(\tau),\tau)
=u_0(\alpha)
+\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
\left[
\mathcal A
-\frac1P\partial_\lambda(P\Theta)
\right]_{(X(s),s)}ds.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

This equation is exact but implicit because the integrand is generated by the
coupled reduced/full state.

---

## 9. Characteristic integral form for $\Theta$

The exact general equation is

$$
D_\tau\Theta
+2\Theta\partial_\lambda u
=2\Psi-\frac1P\partial_\lambda(PC_3).
$$

Define the source

$$
\boxed{
S_\Theta
=2\Psi-\frac1P\partial_\lambda(PC_3).
}
$$

Along $X(s)$,

$$
\frac{d\Theta}{ds}
+2(\partial_\lambda u)\Theta
=S_\Theta.
$$

Using the integrating factor $e^{2I}$ gives

$$
\boxed{
\Theta(X(\tau),\tau)
=e^{-2I(\tau;\alpha)}
\left[
\Theta_0(\alpha)
+\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
 e^{2I(s;\alpha)}
 S_\Theta(X(s),s)\,ds
\right].
}
$$

Because

$$
\frac{P(X(s),s)}{P_0(\alpha)}=e^{-I(s;\alpha)},
$$

an equivalent form, where $P_0(\alpha)>0$, is

$$
\boxed{
\Theta(X(\tau),\tau)
=\left[\frac{P(X(\tau),\tau)}{P_0(\alpha)}\right]^2
\left[
\Theta_0(\alpha)
+\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
\left(\frac{P_0(\alpha)}{P(X(s),s)}\right)^2
S_\Theta(X(s),s)\,ds
\right].
}
$$

Hence the $\Theta$ equation also has a complete exact integral form. The
remaining issue is not integration; it is obtaining the physically generated
$C_3$ and $\Psi$, which can either be propagated through higher reduced moments
or evaluated directly from the finite LJ trajectories.

## 10. Phase-space characteristic representation

If the exact projected conditional acceleration field $A(\lambda,c,\tau)$ is
known and sufficiently smooth, define phase-space characteristics

$$
\boxed{
\dot\Lambda=C,
\qquad
\dot C=A(\Lambda,C,\tau).
}
$$

Expanding

$$
\partial_\tau F+\partial_\lambda(cF)+\partial_c(AF)=0
$$

gives along such a characteristic

$$
\frac{dF}{d\tau}
=-F\,\partial_c A.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{
F(\Lambda(\tau),C(\tau),\tau)
=F_0(\Lambda_0,C_0)
\exp\left[-\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
\partial_cA(\Lambda(s),C(s),s)\,ds\right].
}
$$

This is exact for the projected kinetic equation once the true $A$ is supplied.
For the present chain, the full-flow push-forward formula in Section 3 is more
fundamental because it evaluates the projection without pretending that $A$ is
an autonomous one-point constitutive law.

The mean physical spacing is

\boxed{
\bar a(\tau)
=\frac{a_0}{M}\sum_i
\int
\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).

For the active nearest-neighbour LJ energy,

\int
[\phi(\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0))-\phi(1)]
\,\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

These are exactly equivalent to the $P$-weighted formulas when the same
projected density is used.

## 12. Exact path-integral form of local and specimen first passage

For one spacing trajectory define

$$
\tau_i^c(\Gamma_0)
=\inf\{\tau\ge\tau_0:\Lambda_i(\tau;\Gamma_0)\ge\lambda_c\}.
$$

The local survivor fraction/ensemble probability is

$$
\boxed{
S_{\rm local}(\tau)
=\frac1M\sum_i
\int
\mathbf 1\!\left[
\sup_{s\in[\tau_0,\tau]}
\Lambda_i(s;\Gamma_0)<\lambda_c
\right]
\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
F_{\rm ci}^{\rm local}(\tau)=1-S_{\rm local}(\tau).
}
$$

For a specimen realization, first initiation is the first failure among all
represented spacings. Hence the exact ensemble survival expression is

$$
\boxed{
S_{\rm spec}(\tau)
=\int
\mathbf 1\!\left[
\max_{1\le i\le M}
\sup_{s\in[\tau_0,\tau]}
\Lambda_i(s;\Gamma_0)<\lambda_c
\right]
\mu_0(d\Gamma_0).
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
F_{\rm ci}^{\rm spec}(\tau)=1-S_{\rm spec}(\tau).
}
$$

No product of independent atomic/cell/FEM probabilities is required. Spatial
correlation is automatically retained if it is present in the full-state flow
and in $\mu_0$.

The unresolved specimen-scale problem is therefore sharpened:

$$
\boxed{
\text{not ``find a survival formula'', but determine/validate the physical }
\mu_0\text{ and represented correlation scale.}
}
$$

## 13. G3 in integral form

For the current conservative baseline,

$$
\boxed{
D_{\rm irr}(\tau)=0.
}
$$

If a future physically derived irreversible power
$\dot D_{\rm irr}^*(\Gamma,\tau)\ge0$ is added to the microscopic mechanics,
then each trajectory has

$$
\boxed{
D_{\rm irr}^*(\tau;\Gamma_0)
=\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}
\dot D_{\rm irr}^*(\Phi_{s,\tau_0}^{q}(\Gamma_0),s)\,ds.
}
$$

Its ensemble/spatially appropriate average is obtained by integrating this
trajectory functional against the declared initial measure. This is a formal
exact integral once the irreversible microscopic power is physically specified;
the present theory does not invent that power.

## 14. Final mathematical conclusion

The active 1D theory has two valid levels of solution.

### Reduced local differential level

$$
\boxed{
(P,u,\Theta)
\quad\text{obey exact but non-autonomous hierarchical balance equations.}
}
$$

At this level, closing only on $(P,u,\Theta)$ requires additional assumptions or
higher statistics.

### Full-flow integral level

Given

$$
\boxed{
\{m,n,M,q(\tau),\Gamma_0\ \text{or}\ \mu_0\},
}
$$

the deterministic LJ flow $\Phi^q$ determines

$$
\boxed{
F,\ P,\ u,\ \Theta,\ C_3,\ \Psi,\ \bar a,\ \bar U,
\ S_{\rm local},\ S_{\rm spec}
}
$$

through the explicit projection integrals above.

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\text{lack of a three-field closure}
\neq
\text{lack of an exact integral solution representation}.
}
$$

The remaining blockers are physical rather than algebraic:

1. determine a real irreversible mechanism for G3;
2. justify the initial/specimen measure $\mu_0$ and correlation scale;
3. bridge microscopic time scales to laboratory fatigue cycling;
4. validate against experiment.
