\section*{Local correlation hierarchy and reduced-state sufficiency}
For a deterministic initial microscopic state, the initial one-point spacing and phase-space measures are generated exactly rather than prescribed as named probability families. However, the one-point state is not generally autonomous because the interior spacing acceleration satisfies
\begin{equation}
\ddot\lambda_i
=\phi'(\lambda_{i+1})-2\phi'(\lambda_i)+\phi'(\lambda_{i-1}).
\end{equation}
A directed nearest-neighbor pair measure therefore supplies the instantaneous neighbor-force averages entering the one-point acceleration source. This does not imply exact pair closure. The transport of the pair $(i,i+1)$ requires the outside spacings $\lambda_{i-1}$ and $\lambda_{i+2}$, so its acceleration flux depends on contiguous triplet information. The exact local hierarchy consequently has the schematic form
\begin{equation}
F_1\leftarrow F_2\leftarrow F_3\leftarrow\cdots.
\end{equation}
Nearest-neighbor mechanics makes the hierarchy local but does not make $F_2$ an autonomous state.

A deterministic counterexample was constructed with zero initial rates and zero external force. Let
\begin{equation}
A=0.99,\qquad B=1.01,\qquad C=1.02,
\end{equation}
and compare the ordered spacing states
\begin{equation}
(A,A,A,B,A,C)
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
(A,A,B,A,A,C).
\end{equation}
They have the same one-point spacing measure, the same one-point phase-space measure, the same first and last spacing, and the same directed adjacent-pair counts: $(A,A)$ twice and $(A,B)$, $(B,A)$, and $(A,C)$ once each. Their triplet measures differ. Under the same fixed-left, zero-force-right generalized-LJ dynamics with $m=12.19$ and $n=6$, the later one-point spacing distributions separate. At $\tau=1$, the empirical Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance is
\begin{equation}
D_{KS}=0.3333333333,
\end{equation}
and the maximum difference between sorted spacing samples is approximately
\begin{equation}
5.47\times10^{-3}.
\end{equation}
Hence, for arbitrary ordered initial chains,
\begin{equation}
(P_0,F_0,F_{2,0})+\sigma(0:t)\not\Rightarrow P(t).
\end{equation}
This does not invalidate the probability reduction. It identifies the next problem: determine the smallest local block order that predicts the fatigue observables of interest to a controlled tolerance, rather than declaring an exact low-order closure without evidence.
