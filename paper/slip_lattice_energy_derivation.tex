Deterministic Calibration and a Probabilistic Spacing Hypothesis}
\author{Donghyeok Kim \and Minsoo Kang \and Junhyeok Park}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We derive one intrinsic conservative energy for a reduced single-crystal
fatigue state with normal layer spacing $a$ and one crystallographic registry
$s$.  A row--row interaction $W(d,s)$ is only a kernel: the active local
lattice potential is the multiplicity-free sum $U_0(a,s)=\sum_{k\ge1}W(ka,s)$,
with the same collective $s$ at every layer.  Mellin transformation and
Poisson summation yield an exact Bessel--Lambert representation of the new
two-index lattice sum $H_q$.  For the 12--6 case its half-integer Bessel terms
reduce exactly to verified polylogarithms.  Normal opening and slip are then
an identity decomposition of the same $U_0$, so they are not double counted.
Uniaxial tensile stress enters only as generalized driving forces in a
two-coordinate Smoluchowski equation.  The four governing observables are
mean spacing, mean intrinsic energy, cumulative irreversible dissipation, and
normalization/survival.  Plasticity requires a residual transition of the
unwrapped slip-well index; crack initiation is first passage through the
negative-curvature normal barrier, not an empirical energy threshold.  The
present numerical examples are dimensionless mechanism tests, not calibrated
aluminum-life predictions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and fatigue-crack-initiation objective}
\label{sec:scope}

The physical target is repeated uniaxial normal tension of a pure single
crystal.  The microscopic state uses one normal spacing $a$ and one scalar
registry $s$ for a declared slip system.  The probability state space is thus
two-dimensional, but this does not introduce a two-dimensional continuum
stress criterion.  The only applied waveform is uniaxial.  Generalized
Lennard--Jones energetics are retained deliberately so that the landscape is
derived rather than looked up.  EAM and DFT are future quantitative checks,
not governing potentials in this theory.

\section{Definitions and counting convention}
\label{sec:counting}

Consider one reference row and rows at normal distances $a,2a,3a,\ldots$.
Within every row the tangential repeat is $b$.  The scalar $s$ is one
collective relative registry; it is neither multiplied by the normal-layer
index nor independently redrawn for each layer.  Once one reference layer has
been chosen, its interaction with layer $k$ occurs once.  Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{U_0(a,s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}W(ka,s),}
 \label{eq:U0count}
\end{equation}
with no multiplicity before $W$.  In contrast, $\sum_k kW(ka,s)$ counts all
cross-interface layer pairs of two half-spaces.  That is a distinct interface
energy and is not the local-state convention.  Similarly $W(ka,ks)$ would
assign a different registry to every layer and is excluded by the definition
of the collective coordinate.

\section{Generalized Lennard--Jones $m$--$n$ pair potential}
\label{sec:pair}

We use the well-depth convention
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{v_{m,n}(r)=C_{m,n}\epsLJ
 \left[\left(\frac{\sigLJ}{r}\right)^m-
       \left(\frac{\sigLJ}{r}\right)^n\right],\qquad
 C_{m,n}=\frac{m}{m-n}\left(\frac mn\right)^{n/(m-n)}.}
 \label{eq:pair}
\end{equation}
It gives $\min v=-\epsLJ$ at
$r_e=\sigLJ(m/n)^{1/(m-n)}$.  For $m=12,n=6$, $C_{12,6}=4$, so the usual
$4\epsLJ$ convention is recovered rather than silently mixed with a different
coefficient definition.  The full multilayer absolute energy below requires
\begin{equation}\boxed{m>n>2.}\label{eq:convergence}
\end{equation}
The weaker condition $q>1$ suffices for an individual row kernel.

\section{Single-row kernel $W(d,s)$}
\label{sec:row}

For row separation $d$ and site index $p\in\mathbb Z$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{W_{m,n}(d,s)=\sum_{p=-\infty}^{\infty}
 v_{m,n}\!\left(\sqrt{d^2+(pb+s)^2}\right).}
 \label{eq:W}
\end{equation}
Put $\delta=s/b$, $\eta=d/b$ and, for $q>1$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{B_q(\delta,\eta)=\sum_{p=-\infty}^{\infty}
 [(p+\delta)^2+\eta^2]^{-q/2}.}
 \label{eq:Bdirect}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 W_{m,n}=C_{m,n}\epsLJ\left[
 (\sigLJ/b)^mB_m-(\sigLJ/b)^nB_n\right].
 \label{eq:WB}
\end{equation}

\section{Exact Mellin--Poisson--Bessel representation of $W$}
\label{sec:mellin}

For $x>0$ and $\operatorname{Re}\alpha>0$, the Gamma integral gives
\begin{equation}
 x^{-\alpha}=\frac1{\Gamma(\alpha)}
 \int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}e^{-xt}\dd t.
 \label{eq:mellin}
\end{equation}
Applying it termwise to Eq.~\eqref{eq:Bdirect} is justified by Tonelli's
theorem for real $q>1$.  Shifted Gaussian Poisson summation gives
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{p\in\mathbb Z}e^{-t(p+\delta)^2}
 =\sqrt{\frac\pi t}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}
 e^{-\pi^2\ell^2/t}e^{2\pi i\ell\delta}.
 \label{eq:poisson}
\end{equation}
The required nonzero reciprocal integral follows from the defining identity
for the modified Bessel function of the second kind~\cite{DLMFBessel},
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{K_\nu(x)=\frac12\int_0^\infty
 u^{\nu-1}\exp\!\left[-\frac x2(u+u^{-1})\right]\dd u,}
 \label{eq:Kdef}
\end{equation}
because
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^\infty t^{\nu-1}e^{-\beta t-\gamma/t}\dd t
 =2(\gamma/\beta)^{\nu/2}K_\nu(2\sqrt{\beta\gamma}).
\end{equation}
Separating the $\ell=0$ mode and combining $\pm\ell$ yields the exact identity
\begin{equation}
\boxed{B_q(\delta,\eta)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{\Gamma(q/2)}\left[
 \Gamma\!\left(\frac{q-1}{2}\right)\eta^{1-q}
 +4\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\cos(2\pi\ell\delta)
 \left(\frac{\pi\ell}{\eta}\right)^{(q-1)/2}
 K_{(q-1)/2}(2\pi\ell\eta)\right].}
\label{eq:Bbessel}
\end{equation}
No small-slip, harmonic, or Taylor approximation was made.

\section{Multi-layer intrinsic lattice potential}
\label{sec:H}

Now redefine $\eta=a/b$ and introduce
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{H_q(\delta,\eta)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}B_q(\delta,k\eta)
 =\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{p=-\infty}^{\infty}
 [(p+\delta)^2+k^2\eta^2]^{-q/2}.}
 \label{eq:Hdirect}
\end{equation}
This two-index absolute sum converges for $q>2$, as follows by comparison
with the radial integral of $R^{-q}$ over a two-dimensional lattice.  The
intrinsic potential is therefore
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 U_0(a,s)&=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{p=-\infty}^{\infty}
 v_{m,n}\!\left(\sqrt{k^2a^2+(pb+s)^2}\right)\\
 &=C_{m,n}\epsLJ\left[(\sigLJ/b)^mH_m(\delta,\eta)-
 (\sigLJ/b)^nH_n(\delta,\eta)\right].
 \end{aligned}}
 \label{eq:U0H}
\end{equation}
It depends only on atomic positions.  External stress work is absent.

\section{Exact $H_q$ series}

Let $\nu_q=(q-1)/2$.  The zero Fourier modes sum as
$\sum_{k\ge1}(k\eta)^{1-q}=\eta^{1-q}\zeta(q-1)$.  The nonzero modes give the
Bessel--Lambert series
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\mathcal K_\nu(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-\nu}K_\nu(kx).}
 \label{eq:Klambert}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\boxed{H_q=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{\Gamma(q/2)}\left[
 \Gamma(\nu_q)\eta^{1-q}\zeta(q-1)
 +4\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\cos(2\pi\ell\delta)
 \left(\frac{\pi\ell}{\eta}\right)^{\nu_q}
 \mathcal K_{\nu_q}(2\pi\ell\eta)\right].}
\label{eq:Hexact}
\end{equation}
Absolute convergence permits the reordering.  Exponential decay of $K_\nu$
makes the reciprocal form rapidly convergent for $\eta>0$; its truncations are
numerical controls, not physical cutoffs.

\section{Normal/slip decomposition from the same $U_0$}
\label{sec:decomp}

For one reference $(a_0,s_0)$, add and subtract $U_0(a,s_0)$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \Delta U_0(a,s)&=U_0(a,s)-U_0(a_0,s_0)\\
 &=\underbrace{U_0(a,s_0)-U_0(a_0,s_0)}_{\Delta U_n(a)}
 +\underbrace{U_0(a,s)-U_0(a,s_0)}_{V_{\rm slip}(a,s)}.
\end{aligned}}
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
This is an exact identity.  It replaces the former practice of treating a
collinear $U_\infty$ and a two-row $W$ as independent candidates.  Those
reduced geometries may be archived, but are not added to form the present
fundamental energy.

\section{Slip-excess $\Delta H_q$}

With $\delta_0=s_0/b$, the zero mode cancels exactly:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \Delta H_q={4\sqrt\pi\over\Gamma(q/2)}
 \sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}&[\cos(2\pi\ell\delta)-
 \cos(2\pi\ell\delta_0)]
 \left({\pi\ell\over\eta}\right)^{\nu_q}\\[-0.2em]
 &\times\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-\nu_q}
 K_{\nu_q}(2\pi\ell k\eta).
\end{aligned}}
\label{eq:DeltaH}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 V_{\rm slip}=C_{m,n}\epsLJ[(\sigLJ/b)^m\Delta H_m-
 (\sigLJ/b)^n\Delta H_n].
 \label{eq:Vslip}
\end{equation}
At $\delta=\delta_0$ every bracket is zero, proving
$V_{\rm slip}(a,s_0)=0$.  Replacing $\delta$ by $\delta+1$ leaves every cosine
unchanged, proving period $b$.  If $s_0=0$,
$\cos(2\pi\ell\delta)-1=-2\sin^2(\pi\ell\delta)$.

\section{LJ 12--6 polylogarithm special case}
\label{sec:polylog}

The independently expanded half-integer functions are
\begin{align}
 K_{5/2}(x)&=\sqrt{\pi/(2x)}e^{-x}(1+3x^{-1}+3x^{-2}),\\
 K_{11/2}(x)&=\sqrt{\pi/(2x)}e^{-x}(1+15x^{-1}+105x^{-2}
 +420x^{-3}+945x^{-4}+945x^{-5}).
\end{align}
In $k^{-\nu}K_\nu(kx)$, the square-root adds $k^{-1/2}$.
Using $\sum_{k\ge1}e^{-kx}/k^r=\operatorname{Li}_r(e^{-x})$ gives
\begin{align}
\mathcal K_{5/2}(x)&=\sqrt{\pi/(2x)}[\operatorname{Li}_3(e^{-x})
 +3x^{-1}\operatorname{Li}_4(e^{-x})+3x^{-2}\operatorname{Li}_5(e^{-x})],
 \label{eq:poly6}\\
\mathcal K_{11/2}(x)&=\sqrt{\pi/(2x)}[\operatorname{Li}_6(e^{-x})
 +15x^{-1}\operatorname{Li}_7(e^{-x})+105x^{-2}\operatorname{Li}_8(e^{-x})\notag\\
&\quad+420x^{-3}\operatorname{Li}_9(e^{-x})
 +945x^{-4}\operatorname{Li}_{10}(e^{-x})
 +945x^{-5}\operatorname{Li}_{11}(e^{-x})].
 \label{eq:poly12}
\end{align}
Substitution into Eq.~\eqref{eq:Hexact} is the explicit polylog form.  The
coefficients were generated from the exact half-integer formula and checked
against both unsimplified Bessel sums and real-space double sums.

\section{Unwrapped slip and plastic deformation}
\label{sec:plastic}

The periodic energy does not justify folding the state history.  Write
\begin{equation}\boxed{s=s_0+zb+\widetilde s,\qquad z\in\mathbb Z,}
\label{eq:unwrapped}\end{equation}
and let $\mathcal W_z$ be well $z$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 p_z=\iint_{\mathcal W_z}P\dd a\dd s,qquad
 \langle z\rangle=\sum_z zp_z.
\end{equation}
For an independently specified homogenization thickness,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\gamma_p={b\over h_{\rm slip}}\langle z\rangle,qquad
 \varepsilon_p=M{b\over h_{\rm slip}}\langle z\rangle.}
 \label{eq:plastic-strain}
\end{equation}
Intrawell lag is not plasticity.  The reduced operational criterion is a
residual $\Delta\langle z\rangle\ne0$ after unloading and relaxation.

\section{Tensile-driving probability dynamics}
\label{sec:dynamics}

The only applied load is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\sigma(t)=\sigma_m+\sigma_a\sin\omega t,\qquad
 Q_a=A_0\sigma(t),\quad Q_s=A_0M\sigma(t).}
 \label{eq:driving}
\end{equation}
$M=(\bm e\cdot\bm n)(\bm e\cdot\bm d)$ is the signed resolved-slip
projection for one declared slip plane and direction.  There is no independent
shear fatigue input.

Eliminating isothermal bath degrees of freedom after velocity relaxation gives
$\dot q_i=-M_i(\partial_{q_i}U_0-Q_i)+\sqrt{2\kB TM_i}\,\xi_i$.
The fluctuation--dissipation coefficient is fixed, and the Fokker--Planck
equation is~\cite{Risken,Gardiner}
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\partial_tP=-\partial_aJ_a-\partial_sJ_s,}
 \label{eq:smol}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{J_a=-M_a[P(\partial_aU_0-Q_a)+\kB T\partial_aP],\quad
 J_s=-M_s[P(\partial_sU_0-Q_s)+\kB T\partial_sP].}
 \label{eq:currents}
\end{equation}
This PDE is the underlying evolution law; it is not counted as one of the
four observable equations below.

\section{The four governing equations}
\label{sec:four}

For a closed bonded domain $\Omega_b$, the named equations are
\begin{align}
\text{G1:}\quad&\boxed{\bar a(t)=\iint_{\Omega_b}aP\dd a\dd s,}
\label{eq:G1}\\
\text{G2:}\quad&\boxed{\bar U(t)=\iint_{\Omega_b}
[U_0(a,s)-U_0(a_0,s_0)]P\dd a\dd s,}
\label{eq:G2}\\
\text{G3:}\quad&\boxed{E_{\rm hyst}(t)=\int_0^t\dot D_{\rm irr}(t')\dd t',\quad
\dot D_{\rm irr}=\iint_{\Omega_b}\left[{J_a^2\over M_aP}+{J_s^2\over M_sP}\right]
\dd a\dd s\ge0,}
\label{eq:G3}\\
\text{G4:}\quad&\boxed{\iint_{\Omega_b}P(a,s,t)\dd a\dd s=1.}
\label{eq:G4}
\end{align}
With no-flux boundaries, integration by parts gives
$\dd\bar a/\dd t=\iint J_a\dd a\dd s$ and
\begin{equation}
 {\dd\bar U\over\dd t}=\iint(\partial_aU_0J_a+
 \partial_sU_0J_s)\dd a\dd s.
\end{equation}
The omitted boundary terms must be restored for absorption.  Because
$U_0(a,s+b)=U_0(a,s)$, an equivalent-well transition can lower $\bar U$;
therefore $\bar U$ is not a cumulative damage variable.

\section{Hysteresis and irreversible energy accumulation}

The nonequilibrium free energy
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F[P,t]=\iint P[U_0-Q_a a-Q_s s+\kB T\ln(P/P_*)]\dd a\dd s
\end{equation}
satisfies, for no-flux or periodic boundaries,
\begin{equation}
 {\dd\mathcal F\over\dd t}=-\dot Q_a\bar a-\dot Q_s\bar s-
 \dot D_{\rm irr}.
\end{equation}
Thus finite mobility produces phase lag because current needs finite time to
redistribute $P$; diffusion alone is not the cause.  In a quasistatic closed
cycle the conditional distribution returns to the same state and loop area
tends to zero.  For cycle $N$,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta E_{{\rm hyst},N}=\int_{(N-1)T_c}^{NT_c}\dot D_{\rm irr}\dd t,qquad
 E_{\rm hyst}^{(N)}=\sum_{j=1}^N\Delta E_{{\rm hyst},j}.
\end{equation}
$E_{\rm hyst}$ is total irreversible/hysteretic dissipation.  It may include
heat transferred to eliminated modes as well as microstructural irreversible
work, so it is tracked as history but not equated automatically with stored
damage energy.

\section{Crack-initiation absorbing boundary and first passage}
\label{sec:crack}

At fixed $s,t$, stationary normal states solve the full-energy force balance
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\partial_aU_0(a^\ddagger,s)=Q_a(t),\qquad
 \partial_a^2U_0(a^\ddagger,s)<0.}
 \label{eq:normal-barrier}
\end{equation}
The positive-curvature root is the stable bonded state; the outer
negative-curvature root defines the absorbing barrier.  With absorption,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{S(t)=\iint_{\Omega_b(t)}P\dd a\dd s\le1,qquad
 P_{\rm crack}(t)=1-S(t).}
 \label{eq:G4escape}
\end{equation}
If the moving boundary is a graph $a=a^\ddagger(s,t)$ and registry edges are
periodic or no-flux, Reynolds transport gives the relative outflux
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{-\dot S=\int\left[J_a-J_s\partial_sa^\ddagger-
 P\partial_ta^\ddagger\right]_{a=a^\ddagger}\dd s.}
 \label{eq:movingflux}
\end{equation}
For an ideal absorbing boundary $P=0$, its motion term vanishes.  A finite-grid
radiation implementation must retain it.  Neither an arbitrary distance nor
$E_{\rm hyst}>E_c$ defines fracture.

\section{Model limitations and future quantitative extension}

The exact sums do not determine the representative area $A_0$, mobilities,
memory time, active slip system, or $h_{\rm slip}$.  One collective registry
does not contain dislocation multiplication, hardening, backstress, or
multiple-slip interactions.  These unresolved quantities prevent a
quantitative aluminum fatigue-life claim.  A future FCC/EAM/DFT calculation
may validate or extend the generalized-LJ landscape, but it does not replace
the current analytical governing energy~\cite{DawBaskes}.  Likewise, a CAD/FEM mesh supplies
geometry and scalar normal stress only; it does not add shear or multiaxial
constitutive physics.

\section*{\koreanfont 한국어 연구 요약}

이 연구의 활성 위치에너지는 단일 row kernel $W$ 자체가 아니라
\[
 U_0(a,s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}W(ka,s)
\]
이다. 기준 layer와 $a,2a,3a,\ldots$ 떨어진 각 layer의 상호작용을 한 번씩
세므로 앞에 multiplicity $k$가 없다. $\sum kW(ka,s)$는 두 half-space 사이의
모든 layer pair를 세는 별도 interface convention이다. 또한 $s$는 하나의
collective/unwrapped slip 좌표이므로 모든 $k$에 동일하며 $ks$나 $js$를 쓰지
않는다.

$B_q$의 기존 Mellin--Poisson--Bessel 유도를 보존한 채 다시 $k$에 대해
합하면 식~\eqref{eq:Hexact}의 정확한 $H_q$를 얻는다. 이는 Taylor 또는
harmonic 근사가 아니다. 전체 absolute double sum 때문에 $m>n>2$가
필요하고, 단일 row에는 $q>1$이면 충분하다. 12--6에서는 $K_{5/2}$와
$K_{11/2}$가 유한다항식으로 닫혀 식~\eqref{eq:poly6}--\eqref{eq:poly12}의
polylog 식이 된다. 계수는 직접 이중합과 Bessel 합을 서로 비교하여 검산했다.

normal opening과 slip은 서로 다른 에너지를 더한 것이 아니다. 동일한 $U_0$에
$U_0(a,s_0)$를 더하고 빼서 식~\eqref{eq:decomp}처럼 $\Delta U_n$과
$V_{\rm slip}$으로 정확히 분해한다. 외부응력의 일은 intrinsic $U_0$에 넣지
않고, 단축 인장응력 하나로부터 $Q_a=A_0\sigma$와 $Q_s=A_0M\sigma$만
확률방정식에 넣는다. 독립 shear 피로하중은 없다.

공식적인 4 governing equations는 G1 평균 층간거리, G2 평균 intrinsic
초과에너지, G3 누적 비가역 dissipation $E_{\rm hyst}$, G4 정규화이다.
$U_0$가 $s$에 대해 주기적이므로 slip 후 평균 lattice energy는 다시 낮아질 수
있다. 반면 $E_{\rm hyst}=\int\dot D_{\rm irr}\dd t$는 단조 비감소하지만,
그 전체가 재료에 저장된 damage energy라고 해석하지 않는다.

소성은 단순한 phase lag가 아니다. $s=s_0+zb+\widetilde s$로 펼쳐 저장하고,
unloading과 relaxation 뒤에도 $\Delta\langle z\rangle\ne0$일 때만 잔류
소성변형으로 정의한다. 균열개시는 임의 $a_c$ 또는 $E_c$가 아니라
$\partial_aU_0=Q_a$, $\partial_a^2U_0<0$인 외측 metastable barrier를
통과하는 확률유출로 정의한다. 경계가 움직이면 식~\eqref{eq:movingflux}의
상대 flux를 사용한다.

현재 결과는 수학적/무차원 mechanism 검증이다. $A_0$, mobility, memory,
